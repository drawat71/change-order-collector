# Scaffold review — before the build

The idea addresses a credible, costly handoff: undocumented extra work becomes a payment dispute. The differentiator should be the evidence-to-approval-to-invoice chain, with PayPal closing the loop. Today the scaffold demonstrates only invoice API plumbing. A judge cannot create a change order, see a photo, review an AI draft, approve it, or verify that billing follows approval. It would score poorly on design, implementation breadth, innovation, and presentation despite a useful mock proof. Impact is plausible but unquantified: avoid invented savings claims and validate with contractors.

For Best Use of PayPal + AI, a canned summary and mocked invoice alone are weak evidence. Show how the draft stays faithful to the contractor's scope, the homeowner explicitly authorizes a specific amount, and the approved record drives PayPal invoicing and paid reconciliation. The local deterministic demo is not generative AI; a real model-backed run, evaluated for accuracy and disclosed, and a real PayPal sandbox payment are needed to make a convincing category claim. Do not imply the AI reads photos unless it actually does.

Correctness and security gaps: webhook verification currently fails open when the webhook ID is absent; an attacker could forge paid events on an exposed listener. The JSON store lacks cross-process transactions, event deduplication and robust recovery; unknown invoice IDs can create records. PayPal create/send failures, retries, duplicate invoices and payment races need careful handling. No authentication, authorization, CSRF protection, encrypted storage, upload controls, retention policy or trustworthy identity verification exists. An approval link alone is a bearer token, not proof of identity or a legal signature. Treat the scaffold as a local prototype, not a production billing system.

Demo risks: no sandbox credentials or verified external webhook yet; OAuth, invoice requirements, email delivery and sandbox payment remain unproven. Network and model latency, incorrect model output, photo upload failures, and switching between contractor and homeowner views can derail a short video. Keep a repeatable offline demo, visible status transitions, and a concise backup recording, but label simulation honestly. Validate a real sandbox end to end before making live claims.
